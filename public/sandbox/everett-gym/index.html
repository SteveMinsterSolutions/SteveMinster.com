Everett's Home Gym Plan

Prepared by Steve · October 21, 2026

The recommendation

Put a short power rack on a homemade 8′ × 6′ platform in the southwest corner of the bonus room. It's the strongest spot on the second floor, and the ceiling clears with room to spare.

~$1,145total, before tax and shipping
~4 hrsto build the platform
8″+headroom over the rack

Why the bonus room corner

  • Not Everett's room. Bedroom #3 sits over the garage, a long span with nothing underneath. Fine for furniture, but bouncy and loud under a loaded bar.
  • This corner sits on the house's support. The Drees plans show the bonus room floor joists resting on the beam and columns between the living and dining rooms. The weight goes almost straight down into posts instead of out across a span.
  • The ceiling works with a short rack. The knee wall is 82.5″ to the ceiling. A 71″ rack on a 2.25″ platform leaves about 8–12″ to spare, and the ceiling rises to 9′-1″ in the middle of the room.
  • The trade-off. No pull-ups on the rack, and overhead pressing happens out in the middle of the room. For squats, bench, rows and curls, it does the job.

The garage is still the better spot if he ever wants to deadlift heavy or drop weights.

Platform build plan

A weekend job for Steve: glue, screws, and no structural changes. It lifts out if it ever needs to move. The hardest part is carrying everything upstairs, so it needs two people.

Size
8′ wide × 6′ deep × 2.25″ tall
Layers
3/4″ plywood, 3/4″ plywood, 3/4″ rubber mat
Time
3–4 hours, plus 24 hours for glue to cure

Materials

ItemQtyCost
3/4″ plywood, 4×83$150
Rubber stall mat, 4×6, Tractor Supply2$128
Construction adhesive, 28 oz2~$16
1-1/4″ wood screws1 box~$15
Total~$310

Have the store rip one plywood sheet lengthwise into two 24″ × 96″ strips. That's the only cut.

Tools

Drill/driver, caulk gun, tape measure, straightedge. A circular saw only if you rip the sheet yourself.

Steps

  1. Air out the mats first. New stall mats smell strongly of rubber. Leave them in the garage for about a week.
  2. Mark the corner. 8′ along the knee wall from the window wall and 6′ out, about 1/2″ off the baseboards. Build right on the carpet.
  3. Lay layer 1. A full sheet against the knee wall, long side along the wall, with a 24″ strip in front.
  4. Glue. Zigzag beads of adhesive across all of layer 1, about 8″ apart.
  5. Lay layer 2 with the seams offset. The 24″ strip goes against the wall and the full sheet in front, so the seams don't line up.
  6. Screw the layers together about every 10″, and 1″ in from every edge. The screws stay in the platform, not the house's floor.
  7. Let it cure for 24 hours.
  8. Lay the two mats side by side on top. Each weighs 94 lb.
  9. Assemble the rack on the platform, about 6″ off the knee wall. Check ceiling clearance with the empty bar before adding weight.

DIY or carpenter? Likely DIY. A handyman could do it in a half day if needed, probably for a few hundred dollars.

House rules

The platform handles the weight; these rules handle the noise and impact over the living room.

  • Lower it, never drop it. Dropped iron plates are what crack ceilings below.
  • Safety bars set every session, just below his squat depth and just above his chest on bench, so a missed rep lands on the rack.
  • Overhead pressing at the front of the platform, under the flat 9′-1″ ceiling.
  • No pull-ups on the rack. Not enough headroom at the wall.
  • Plates go back on the rack's plate holders, not loose on the carpet.

Before we buy

  • Confirm the rack's footprint orientation with Titan (listed as 57″ × 50″ overall).
  • Re-measure the corner to the baseboards.
  • Confirm the knee wall is 82.5″ right where the rack will stand.
  • Check the Keppi bench sits centered between the rack uprights (42″ inside width).
  • Pick up the mats a week before building so they can air out.
